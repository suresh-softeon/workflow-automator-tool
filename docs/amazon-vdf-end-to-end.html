Amazon Vendor Direct Fulfillment (VDF)

The whole journey of an order, from the customer's click to the parcel on the doorstep

1. The one-minute story

Think of Amazon as a shop window and your company as the warehouse behind it.

  • A customer buys a product on Amazon's website.
  • Amazon does not hold that product. You do.
  • So Amazon sends you a Purchase Order (PO): "Please send this item to this customer."
  • You check the PO, say yes or no, ship the parcel, and tell Amazon the tracking number.

2. Why do WE pull the order and create it in DOMS?

Amazon owns the customer's order. You own the fulfillment. Amazon can't ship what it doesn't have.

QuestionSimple answer
Why doesn't Amazon do everything?The stock, the warehouse and the carriers are yours. Amazon has no access to them.
Why create the order in DOMS?The Amazon PO is only a request. Your systems need their own order to reserve stock, plan shipping, invoice and report.
Why do we "pull" it?Amazon publishes new POs through an API and you fetch them regularly. It works the same way for every vendor.
Why do we have to acknowledge?Only you know whether you can really ship (stock, valid SKU). Amazon wants a yes or no, normally within 24 hours.
In one line: Amazon sells, you fulfill. The API is how Amazon hands you the sale, and DOMS is how you turn it into a shipment.

4. End-to-end flow (the big picture)

5. The flow, step by step

1
Customer buys on Amazon.
Amazon knows you stock this item, so it creates a Purchase Order (PO) for you.
2
DOMS asks Amazon for new orders (getOrders).
It is like checking a mailbox. DOMS asks for POs created in a time window and goes through them page by page.
3
DOMS creates the order on your side.
The PO number (for example 6K9Z13PL), the ship-to address, the product and the quantity are copied into your own order. It is then passed to SAP as an ORDERS05 IDoc.
4
Check: can we really ship this?
Is the item in stock? Is the SKU valid? Is the address serviceable?
5
Send the acknowledgement (submitAcknowledgement).
Amazon expects this within 24 hours.
CodeMeaningDescription to send
00Accept: we will ship it allShipping 100 percent of ordered product
02Reject: we cannot shipA reason, e.g. Canceled due to missing/invalid SKU
The answer is for the whole order, not part of it, and it must list all lines of the PO.
6
Amazon gives you a transactionId.
This is only a receipt that says "we got your message". It does not mean Amazon accepted your answer.
7
DOMS checks the receipt (getTransactionStatus).
StatusMeaning
ProcessingAmazon is still working. Check again shortly.
SuccessAmazon processed it.
FailureAmazon refused it. The response lists the errors.
8
The warehouse picks, packs and ships.
SAP and the WMS reserve the stock, create the delivery and hand the parcel to the carrier.
9
Tell Amazon it shipped.
Send the shipment confirmation with the carrier, tracking number and boxes, through the Shipping API. Labels and packing slips come from the same API.
10
Send the invoice.
Amazon uses it to pay you.

6. What if the order is cancelled on our side?

Amazon has no "cancel order" button in the API. The only way to cancel is to answer the PO with a rejection (02).

Not yet confirmed: whether Amazon accepts a 02 after you already sent a 00. It may return a Failure with an error. Test it in production on a PO you really intend to cancel, and read the result from getTransactionStatus and getOrder.
Best practice: check stock and SKU before sending the first 00. Then you will rarely need to cancel.

7. How to know the result is real

You see...It means...
transactionId onlyAmazon received the request. Nothing is confirmed.
Status SuccessAmazon processed it. Still confirm with getOrder.
Status Failure + errorsAmazon refused it. Read the error and fix it.
getOrder shows the order as cancelled or rejectedThe cancellation is real. Mark it cancelled in DOMS.
mock-TransactionId... or always "Processing"You are in the sandbox. It is a test, and nothing really happened.

8. The same flow as a story (a worked example)

  1. Priya orders a kettle on Amazon on Monday.
  2. Amazon creates PO 6K9Z13PL and publishes it.
  3. DOMS pulls it in the morning and creates the order in SAP.
  4. Stock is available, so DOMS sends 00, which means "we will ship it all".
  5. Amazon returns a transactionId, and DOMS later sees Success.
  6. The warehouse packs the kettle and the carrier collects it.
  7. DOMS sends Amazon the tracking number, then the invoice.
  8. Priya gets her kettle, and Amazon pays you.

Alternative ending: the warehouse finds the kettle is damaged and out of stock. DOMS sends 02 with the reason, checks getOrder, and only then marks the order cancelled.
